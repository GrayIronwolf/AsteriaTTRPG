## Description

A Bloodhunter soon learns that blood is more than life—it is movement. Every heartbeat drives muscles, every pulse fuels strength, and every flowing vein keeps a creature alive. Through Sangramancy, the Bloodhunter can momentarily seize control of that flow, causing the blood within an enemy's body to thicken and resist its natural movement.

This technique is known as **Veinlock**. Rather than inflicting overwhelming pain, the Bloodhunter briefly restricts circulation, causing muscles to stiffen and reactions to slow. The effect is subtle but devastating, robbing even the fastest prey of the speed they rely upon to escape.

Veinlock is most effective against creatures already suffering from open wounds. Fresh blood provides the Sangramancer with a stronger connection to the target, allowing the manipulation to spread more easily through the circulatory system. Veteran Bloodhunters often combine Blood Rite with Veinlock, weakening their prey before closing in for the kill.

The technique is completely ineffective against creatures without circulating blood. Constructs, most undead, oozes, and similar beings simply possess no living blood for the Bloodhunter to command.

## Effects

- As an **Action**, target one creature with circulating blood within **15 metres**.
- Spend **15 MP**, sacrifice **5 HP**, and gain **5 Blood Points (BP)**.
- The target must succeed on a **Constitution Saving Throw** against your **Sangramancy DC**.
- On a failed save, the target becomes **Veinlocked** for **2 Combat Rounds**.
- While Veinlocked, the target:
  - Has its **Movement Speed reduced by 50%**.
  - Cannot take the **Dash** action.
  - Has **Disadvantage** on Dexterity (Acrobatics) checks.
- If the target is currently suffering from a **Bleeding** effect, it has **Disadvantage** on the Constitution Saving Throw.
- A creature may only be affected by one instance of Veinlock at a time.
- Veinlock has **no effect** on creatures without functioning blood unless another Bloodhunter ability specifically states otherwise.

## Lore

Among the Bloodhunter Orders, Veinlock is often the first true display of offensive Sangramancy taught to apprentices. Unlike Blood Rite, which channels one's own blood into a weapon, Veinlock reaches outward, proving that another creature's blood can be manipulated just as easily as one's own.

Many apprentices struggle with the technique at first. They instinctively try to force the blood to stop completely, only to find the connection breaks under such strain. Masters instead teach them to think of a river rather than a dam—slowing the current just enough that the prey begins to stumble under its own weight.

Experienced Bloodhunters know that a fleeing monster is often more dangerous than a cornered one. Veinlock was created not to kill, but to ensure that once the hunt begins, the prey can never outrun the hunter.

> *"Every creature believes its legs will carry it to safety. They forget who commands the blood that moves them."*
