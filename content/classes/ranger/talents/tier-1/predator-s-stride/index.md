# Predator's Stride

## Overview
A Ranger knows that every successful hunt begins by choosing a single quarry. Once a target has been identified, their focus narrows until every footprint, broken branch, disturbed blade of grass, and subtle movement contributes to the pursuit. Predator's Stride represents this unwavering commitment to the hunt.

Upon declaring a creature as their **Prey**, the Ranger instinctively adapts their movements and awareness to that individual. They become quieter, more observant, and better at following signs left behind by their quarry. While the hunt continues, the Ranger is reluctant to abandon their target, trusting patience and persistence over reckless pursuit.

Among the Ranger Lodges, changing prey before the hunt is complete is considered a mark of an inexperienced hunter. A true Ranger commits to the chase until it reaches its natural conclusion.

---

### Effects

#### Declare Prey

Choose one creature within **line of sight** as your **Prey**.

The Prey remains marked for **30 minutes**.

The Ranger cannot voluntarily choose another Prey until:

- The current Prey dies.
- The current Prey becomes unconscious.
- The duration expires.

#### Hunter's Focus

Against your declared Prey, gain:

- **+1 Stealth** checks.
- **+1 Survival** checks made to track the Prey.
- **+1 Perception** checks made to locate the Prey.
- **+1 Investigation** checks involving signs or evidence left by the Prey.

#### Relentless Pursuit

If the Prey attempts to conceal, erase, or disguise its tracks, it has **Disadvantage** on Survival checks made to do so against the Ranger.

#### Limitations

- Only one creature may be designated as your Prey at a time.
- Predator's Stride does not grant bonuses against other creatures.
- This talent does not provide bonuses to attack or damage rolls.
- If the Prey dies or becomes unconscious, the effect immediately ends.

### Lore

Every experienced Ranger remembers their first true hunt. Their mentor would point to a single set of tracks and give one simple instruction: *"Follow."* Days later, the lesson was never about catching the animal—it was about learning commitment.

The greatest hunters are not those who chase the fastest, but those who refuse to abandon the trail. Predator's Stride embodies this philosophy, teaching Rangers that every quarry leaves behind a story for those patient enough to read it.

Across Asteria, Rangers renowned for mastering Predator's Stride are respected as trackers without equal, capable of pursuing their chosen quarry across forests, mountains, deserts, and frozen tundra without ever losing the trail.

---

> *"Do not hunt everything. Hunt one thing, and hunt it until the world itself tells you the chase is over."*  
> — **High Ranger Toren Wolfstride**

## Rank 2
### Description

The Ranger's focus on their chosen quarry becomes unwavering. Once a creature has been declared as the Prey, the Ranger begins to anticipate its behaviour, recognise its habits, and instinctively follow the trail it leaves behind. Every broken twig, disturbed patch of earth, or displaced stone becomes another clue guiding the hunt.

With growing experience, the Ranger learns to move almost as though they are part of the landscape itself. They become increasingly difficult for their chosen quarry to detect, while their understanding of the Prey's movements allows them to close the distance without revealing their presence.

Experienced Rangers often describe Predator's Stride as becoming "one with the hunt," where the pursuit feels less like tracking and more like instinct.

### Lore

Among seasoned Rangers, abandoning a hunt before its conclusion is seen as a failure of discipline. Every pursuit teaches the hunter something new about their quarry, and those lessons are only learned through patience and perseverance.

Rangers who master the second rank of Predator's Stride begin to understand that successful tracking is not simply following footprints—it is understanding the choices their prey is likely to make. Rivers, cliffs, forests, and open plains all influence a creature's path, and an experienced Ranger learns to think as their quarry does.

Within the Ranger Lodges, this stage of training is often called **Walking the Same Trail**, signifying that the hunter and the hunted have become locked in a contest of instinct and endurance.

---

> *"The trail does not reveal where the prey has been. It reveals where it must go next."*  
> — **High Ranger Toren Wolfstride**

## Rank 3
### Description

By the third rank, the Ranger's chosen quarry becomes more than just a target—it becomes the sole focus of the hunt. Every instinct is devoted to anticipating the Prey's movements, allowing the Ranger to stalk with remarkable patience and precision. The wilderness itself seems to reveal the quarry's passage, making it increasingly difficult for the Prey to escape unnoticed.

As the pursuit continues, the Ranger learns to move through natural terrain without allowing the environment to slow the hunt. Dense forests, rocky hills, and uneven ground become little more than obstacles to navigate rather than barriers that interrupt the chase.

Veteran Rangers often say that by this stage, the hunt has already been decided. The only remaining question is how long it will take for the Prey to realise it is no longer the hunter—but the hunted.

### Lore

Achieving the third rank of Predator's Stride marks the transition from skilled tracker to relentless hunter. Rangers at this level no longer chase blindly; they predict. Every choice their quarry makes is weighed against years of experience until following the trail becomes almost instinctive.

Many famous monster hunters throughout Asteria earned their reputations through patience rather than speed. Some pursued dangerous beasts for days across forests, mountains, and frozen wastelands, never once allowing the trail to grow cold. Their success came not from superior strength, but from refusing to let the hunt end unfinished.

Within the Ranger Lodges, instructors often tell apprentices that a true predator is never the fastest creature in the forest—it is the one that never gives up.

---

> *"The greatest hunters are not remembered for how quickly they found their prey, but for the fact that they always did."*  
> — **High Ranger Toren Wolfstride**

## Rank 4
### Description

The Ranger has become a relentless hunter, capable of pursuing a chosen quarry across vast distances without allowing the trail to grow cold. Every sign left behind tells a story, and every decision the Prey makes is anticipated through years of experience. Few creatures can evade a Ranger who has committed themselves to the hunt.

At this level, the Ranger no longer allows natural obstacles to dictate the pace of the pursuit. Rivers, dense forests, rocky terrain, and harsh landscapes become challenges to overcome rather than barriers to progress. The Ranger remains calm, patient, and methodical, always confident that the hunt will eventually end in their favour.

Among the Ranger Lodges, reaching this stage earns the title of **Relentless Hunter**, a mark of respect reserved for those whose determination is matched only by their skill.

### Lore

By the fourth rank, a Ranger's reputation often spreads long before they arrive. Stories tell of hunters who followed a single quarry across entire kingdoms, through blizzards, deserts, and dense forests, never once abandoning the trail. To these Rangers, persistence is every bit as important as skill.

Within the Ranger Lodges, mentors teach that every creature eventually grows tired, becomes careless, or makes a mistake. Predator's Stride is built upon this belief, rewarding patience above all else. The Ranger does not rush the hunt—they simply refuse to let it end unfinished.

Many dangerous beasts have survived stronger warriors, faster hunters, and more powerful mages. Very few have ever escaped a Ranger who has truly committed to the pursuit.

---

> *"The prey may outrun the arrow, but it will never outrun the hunter who refuses to stop walking."*  
> — **High Ranger Toren Wolfstride**

## Rank 5
### Description

The Ranger has become the embodiment of the relentless hunter. Once a creature is declared as the Prey, escaping the pursuit becomes a near-impossible task. Every footprint, broken branch, displaced stone, and subtle scent becomes another clue, allowing the Ranger to remain one step behind their quarry no matter where it flees.

Years of experience have taught the Ranger to think like their prey. They anticipate where it will travel, where it will rest, and how it will attempt to hide. Their movements become almost impossible for the quarry to detect, allowing them to stalk even the most dangerous creatures with unwavering confidence.

Among the Ranger Lodges, those who master Predator's Stride earn the title of **Apex Hunter**, a distinction reserved for Rangers whose hunts are said to end only when they choose them to.

### Lore

Only the greatest Rangers ever master Predator's Stride. Their reputation spreads across kingdoms as hunters capable of pursuing monsters, criminals, and legendary beasts for days without losing the trail. To them, the hunt is never rushed. Every clue is examined, every choice is deliberate, and every step brings them closer to their quarry.

The oldest Ranger texts teach that the true predator is not the strongest creature in the wild, but the one that never abandons the chase. Those who earn the title of Apex Hunter embody this philosophy completely, becoming patient, methodical, and unwavering until their quarry is finally brought to ground.

Many creatures survive because they are stronger than their enemies. Few survive once they have become the chosen Prey of a master Ranger.

---

> *"A predator does not ask whether the hunt will succeed. It simply keeps walking until the answer is standing before it."*  
> — **Grand Ranger Toren Wolfstride, First Apex Hunter**
