# Arcane Pursuit

## Overview
Spellblades are taught that fleeing enemies create openings. Arcane Pursuit was developed to punish opponents who attempt to retreat, reposition, or escape the reach of a blade.

By locking onto the motion of a moving target, the Spellblade floods their body with mana and surges after them with explosive speed. Unlike Arcane Rush, which allows free repositioning, Arcane Pursuit is focused entirely on chasing movement. It does not carry the Spellblade wherever they wish to go; it carries them toward a target that has already begun to move.

At Rank I, Arcane Pursuit allows the Spellblade to close distance against enemies who attempt to flee or reposition. It is a reactive hunting technique, rewarding awareness and timing rather than simple speed.

## Rank 1
### Description

Spellblades are taught that fleeing enemies create openings. Arcane Pursuit was developed to punish opponents who attempt to retreat, reposition, or escape the reach of a blade.

By locking onto the motion of a moving target, the Spellblade floods their body with mana and surges after them with explosive speed. Unlike Arcane Rush, which allows free repositioning, Arcane Pursuit is focused entirely on chasing movement. It does not carry the Spellblade wherever they wish to go; it carries them toward a target that has already begun to move.

At Rank I, Arcane Pursuit allows the Spellblade to close distance against enemies who attempt to flee or reposition. It is a reactive hunting technique, rewarding awareness and timing rather than simple speed.

### Effects

- Spend **20 MP** to activate **Arcane Pursuit**.
- Choose one hostile creature within **10 metres** that has moved this turn.
- Instantly move to the nearest valid unoccupied space adjacent to that creature.
- This movement ignores difficult terrain.
- This movement does not provoke Opportunity Attacks.
- After arriving, you may make **one melee weapon attack** against the chosen target.
- If no valid adjacent space exists, Arcane Pursuit fails.
- If Arcane Pursuit fails, you still spend **10 MP**.
- Arcane Pursuit cannot target creatures that have not moved this turn.

### Lore

Arcane Pursuit was created by Spellblades who refused to let enemies control the distance of battle. Archers, mages, and skirmishers often relied on movement to stay beyond the reach of melee warriors, but Spellblades answered with a technique designed to hunt motion itself.

The earliest masters described Arcane Pursuit as listening to the disturbance left behind by movement. Once a target shifted position, the Spellblade could follow that motion through mana, crossing the distance before the enemy fully escaped.

Among the Orders, apprentices are taught that Arcane Pursuit is not a charge. It is a response. The enemy moves first, and the Spellblade answers.

> *"Run if you wish. Every step only tells me where to follow."*
>
> — Grand Spellblade Aetherion Lux, First Blade of the Infinite Well

## Rank 2
### Description

As a Spellblade's mastery grows, Arcane Pursuit becomes faster, more precise, and capable of following enemies over greater distances. Rather than simply reacting to movement, the Spellblade begins to anticipate it, reading the flow of mana left in an opponent's wake before they have fully completed their reposition.

The burst of speed is almost instantaneous. To spectators, the Spellblade appears to vanish in a flash of arcane light before materialising beside their quarry, denying even the most agile foes the opportunity to escape.

At Rank II, Arcane Pursuit extends its reach and improves its efficiency, allowing the Spellblade to relentlessly pressure enemies who rely on movement to survive. Those who believe a few extra metres will save them quickly discover that distance means little to a determined Spellblade.

### Effects

- Spend **25 MP** to activate **Arcane Pursuit**.
- Choose one hostile creature within **15 metres** that has moved this turn.
- Instantly move to the nearest valid unoccupied space adjacent to that creature.
- This movement ignores difficult terrain.
- This movement does not provoke Opportunity Attacks.
- After arriving, you may make **one melee weapon attack** against the chosen target.
- If no valid adjacent space exists, Arcane Pursuit fails.
- If Arcane Pursuit fails, you still spend **12 MP** (rounded down).
- Arcane Pursuit cannot target creatures that have not moved this turn.
- This rank replaces the effects of **Arcane Pursuit — Rank I**.

### Lore

Within the Spellblade Orders, Rank II is known as the **Hunter's Step**. Apprentices begin to realise that Arcane Pursuit is not powered by speed alone, but by recognising the arcane disturbance created when a creature moves across the battlefield.

Veteran Spellblades often describe the sensation as chasing an echo rather than a body. Every movement leaves behind a fleeting trail of mana that an experienced practitioner can instinctively follow, allowing them to intercept enemies before they can capitalise on their new position.

Many skirmishers and battle mages have learned to fear this technique. The moment they choose to retreat, they also choose to invite the Spellblade into striking distance.

> *"Every step your enemy takes leaves a trail. Learn to follow it, and no one truly escapes."*

> — Grand Spellblade Aetherion Lux, First Blade of the Infinite Well

## Rank 3
### Description

Arcane Pursuit has evolved into one of the Spellblade's most feared battlefield techniques. By reading the flow of mana left behind by moving creatures, the Spellblade can instantly close even greater distances, denying opponents the opportunity to disengage safely.

The pursuit is no longer simply a burst of speed. It becomes an act of prediction, allowing the Spellblade to intercept fleeing enemies before they can establish a new position. To an observer, the Spellblade appears to disappear into a streak of arcane light, emerging beside their target with flawless precision.

At Rank III, Arcane Pursuit allows experienced Spellblades to relentlessly pressure mobile opponents, forcing enemies to think twice before attempting to reposition during combat.

### Effects

- Spend **30 MP** to activate **Arcane Pursuit**.
- Choose one hostile creature within **20 metres** that has moved this turn.
- Instantly move to the nearest valid unoccupied space adjacent to that creature.
- This movement ignores difficult terrain and does not provoke Opportunity Attacks.
- Immediately after arriving, you may make **one melee weapon attack** against the chosen target.
- If no valid adjacent space exists, Arcane Pursuit fails.
- If Arcane Pursuit fails, you still spend **15 MP**.
- Arcane Pursuit cannot target creatures that have not moved this turn.
- This rank replaces the effects of **Arcane Pursuit — Rank II**.

### Lore

Spellblade masters refer to Rank III as the **Relentless Pursuit**, the stage where distance begins to lose its meaning. Practitioners no longer chase an enemy's footsteps; they instinctively follow the lingering currents of mana disturbed by movement itself.

Throughout Asteria's history, countless generals have underestimated this technique, believing a tactical withdrawal would create breathing room against a Spellblade. Instead, they found the arcane warrior already standing beside them before their formation could recover.

Among the Orders, students are reminded that Arcane Pursuit is not about moving faster than an opponent. It is about ensuring that movement itself becomes a liability.

> *"The moment your enemy chooses to flee is the moment they have already been caught."*

> — Grand Spellblade Aetherion Lux, First Blade of the Infinite Well

## Rank 4
### Description

The Spellblade has refined Arcane Pursuit into a technique that borders on supernatural. Rather than merely chasing a moving opponent, they instinctively predict where their quarry will be, allowing them to intercept even the swiftest combatants before they can gain an advantage.

At this level, Arcane Pursuit becomes a terrifying display of battlefield dominance. Every attempted retreat, tactical reposition, or desperate escape risks placing the Spellblade directly beside their target. Those who rely upon mobility quickly discover that every movement creates another opportunity for pursuit.

Veteran Spellblades often describe Rank IV as the point where the hunter no longer follows their prey—they simply arrive where the prey intends to be.

### Effects

- Spend **35 MP** to activate **Arcane Pursuit**.
- Choose one hostile creature within **25 metres** that has moved this turn.
- Instantly move to the nearest valid unoccupied space adjacent to that creature.
- This movement ignores difficult terrain and does not provoke Opportunity Attacks.
- Immediately after arriving, you may make **one melee weapon attack** against the chosen target.
- If the attack hits, the target's **Movement Speed is reduced by 50% until the end of its next turn.**
- If no valid adjacent space exists, Arcane Pursuit fails.
- If Arcane Pursuit fails, you still spend **17 MP** (half the Mana Cost, rounded down).
- Arcane Pursuit cannot target creatures that have not moved this turn.
- This rank replaces the effects of **Arcane Pursuit — Rank III**.

### Lore

Rank IV is known within the Spellblade Orders as the **Hunter's Mark**, not because it brands an enemy with magic, but because few creatures escape once a master Spellblade has chosen to pursue them.

Military records speak of elite Spellblades who specialised in hunting enemy commanders, scouts, and battle mages. Attempts to retreat often proved futile, as the Spellblade would appear beside their target before orders could even be given.

Masters teach that Arcane Pursuit is more than a technique—it is a promise. Once a Spellblade commits to the chase, the battle is no longer fought on the enemy's terms.

> *"Movement creates opportunity. For my enemy, it is the opportunity to be caught."*

> — Grand Spellblade Aetherion Lux, First Blade of the Infinite Well

## Rank 5
### Description

Arcane Pursuit reaches its ultimate form, allowing the Spellblade to hunt even the fastest opponents across the battlefield. Through complete mastery of mana and movement, they no longer react to where an enemy has been—they instinctively intercept where they are attempting to go.

To witnesses, the technique appears impossible. A fleeing opponent takes only a few steps before the Spellblade vanishes in a surge of arcane energy, materialising beside them with blade already in motion. Distance offers no safety, and movement itself becomes a dangerous gamble.

Only the greatest Spellblades ever master Arcane Pursuit to this degree. Those who do become relentless hunters, capable of punishing any enemy who believes they can escape the reach of steel and sorcery.

### Effects

- Spend **40 MP** to activate **Arcane Pursuit**.
- Choose one hostile creature within **30 metres** that has moved this turn.
- Instantly move to the nearest valid unoccupied space adjacent to that creature.
- This movement ignores difficult terrain and does not provoke Opportunity Attacks.
- Immediately after arriving, you may make **one melee weapon attack** against the chosen target.
- If the attack hits, the target's **Movement Speed becomes 0 until the end of its next turn**.
- If no valid adjacent space exists, Arcane Pursuit fails.
- If Arcane Pursuit fails, you still spend **20 MP**.
- Arcane Pursuit cannot target creatures that have not moved this turn.
- This rank replaces the effects of **Arcane Pursuit — Rank IV**.

### Lore

Within the oldest Spellblade Orders, Rank V is known as the **Unbroken Chase**. It represents the pinnacle of pursuit magic, where distance, obstacles, and momentum cease to matter. The Spellblade no longer follows an enemy—they simply deny them the ability to escape.

Stories of the Unbroken Chase are common throughout Asteria. Assassins who vanished into crowded streets, battle mages who blinked across fortifications, and monsters that outran entire companies have all fallen to Spellblades who mastered this technique. Their prey quickly learned that every attempt to flee only hastened the inevitable.

To a master of Arcane Pursuit, movement is not an obstacle to overcome—it is an invitation to strike.

> *"You may outrun a soldier. You may outpace a knight. But once a Spellblade begins the chase, your journey ends where my blade begins."*

> — Grand Spellblade Aetherion Lux, First Blade of the Infinite Well
