# Kelriss

## Overview
🪱 Kelriss, the Bone-Sower

Titles:

The Bone-Sower

Lord of Rot and Renewal

Warden of the Worm

The Verdant Reclaimer

Gravefather of the Soil


Alignment: Neutral
Pantheon: The Pale Dominion
Symbol: A skull sprouting flowers and roots that twist into a spiral.
Sacred Colors: Moss green, soil brown, bone white.
Animal: Carrion beetle, worm, and crow.


---

🌿 Domains

Decay • Earth • Renewal • Fertility • Death • Nature • Transformation

Kelriss governs what happens after Vorneth’s rites are complete. When the body returns to the earth, it becomes part of Kelriss’s domain — the eternal compost from which new life grows.

He is both feared and loved: the god of rot, but also of rebirth through decay. Farmers and druids revere him as much as undertakers do.


---

🪵 Appearance

Kelriss is a towering, skeletal figure draped in vines and fungus.
Half his body is flesh — vibrant and blooming — while the other half is bare bone crawling with insects and roots.
Wherever he steps, moss grows from stone, and flowers bloom from graves.

His voice sounds like wind through hollow wood — deep, whispering, and strangely soothing.

> “All that falls shall feed the roots.”




---

⚰️ Personality & Teachings

Kelriss is patient, cyclical, and utterly unafraid of endings.
He teaches that death is not destruction, but a natural turning — what dies must nourish what comes next.
To withhold decay is to withhold life itself.

His creed:

> “From rot, bloom. From stillness, growth.”



His virtues:
Acceptance • Balance • Patience • Renewal

He despises those who preserve beyond their time — necromancers, mummifiers, and vain nobles who seek immortality.


---

🌾 Temples and Worship

Priest Titles: Rotwardens, Verdant Keepers, Soil-Tenders

Temples:

Often grown from trees or carved into stone beneath moss and fungi.

Floors are layered with living soil — never swept clean.

Bones and skulls are used as vessels for flowers and incense.

Every temple houses a “Feeding Garden” where remains are buried to grow sacred flora.


Offerings: Compost, seeds, bones, and decayed fruit.

Common Rituals:

The Returning Feast: once a year, offerings of spoiled crops are left in the forest to thank Kelriss for renewal.

The Worm’s Blessing: bodies of the dead are laid in natural soil rather than coffins, to “feed the circle.”

The Blooming Vigil: prayers are said over gardens and graveyards during spring to awaken new growth.



---

🧿 Artifacts and Relics

The Verdant Scythe: forged of bone and green steel; it kills instantly but causes flowers to bloom from wounds.

The Rootheart Idol: pulsing seed of divine decay; buried to heal poisoned or barren land.

The Worm Crown: worn by Kelriss’s highest druid-priests; grants the power to commune with the soil and sense the dead below.



---

🌱 Divine Relationships

Deity	Relationship

Tobias	Ally in the cycle of death; Tobias judges, Kelriss transforms. Mutual respect.
Vorneth	Works closely — once the rites are complete, the remains pass to Kelriss’s care.
Nymera	Sees her as distant kin — her souls take flight while his remains root below.
Elyndra	Sibling spirit; both govern rebirth but disagree — she through spirit, he through soil.
Marvok	Blasphemer. Necromancy halts decay — the highest insult to Kelriss’s creed.



---

🪦 Clergy and Faithful

Followers of Kelriss include:

Druids, rangers, and herbalists

Farmers and gardeners

Grave-tenders and bonecarvers

Those who study decomposition and alchemy


They believe every body — plant, animal, or person — must “return to the wheel.” Many of his followers serve in funeral gardens beside Vorneth’s clergy.

Common prayer:

> “Let me fall, and let me feed the earth.”




---

🌍 Legends and Myth

The Garden of Skulls: It’s said Kelriss once grew an entire forest from the bones of a dead god. Each tree in that grove whispers with the god’s memories.

The Worm’s Covenant: He created the first worms from his own blood so that the earth would always hunger for renewal.

The Bloom of Ages: When a great kingdom falls, Kelriss walks its ruins, planting seeds among the ashes.



---

🍃 Role in the World

Kelriss’s worshippers are pragmatic and grounded — not morbid, but reverent.
They tend to cemeteries, bless farmlands, and protect natural decay. Some are undertakers who work hand in hand with Vorneth’s priests, while others wander the wilderness spreading his spores.

To see a patch of blooming flowers on a fresh grave is called “Kelriss’s Gift.”


---

⚖️ In Art and Culture

He is never painted in decay alone — always balanced with beauty: rot beside bloom, skull beside seed.

Funerary gardens often have a statue of Kelriss kneeling, planting a seed into a skull.

Offerings are left in baskets woven from vines and bones.
