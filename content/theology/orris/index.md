# Orris

## Overview
## Titles:
The Silent Shepherd
Keeper of the Ashes
Lord of Final Rites
The Mourner’s Flame
Guardian of Rest


## Symbol: 
A stone urn surrounded by a ring of white lilies, sometimes a burning censer over crossed hands.



---

🕯️ Domains of Worship

Funerals & Burial Rites

Death & Rest

Protection of Graves

Purification & Cleansing

Transition between Life and Death


Vorneth’s clergy and followers oversee every mortal end: the washing of bodies, the lighting of pyres, the building of cairns, and the closing of tombs.
To die unblessed by Vorneth’s hand is to risk wandering as a Lost Soul — a fate feared across all realms.


---

⚖️ Appearance

Vorneth appears as a faceless figure of pale marble wrapped in layered funeral cloth. When he moves, ashes swirl around his feet, and his hands glow faintly gold, like the last light of a candle.

His presence brings quiet peace, never dread — the stillness of a final breath, not the terror of oblivion.

> “There is no horror in ending, only the duty to end well.”




---

🕊️ Personality & Teachings

Where Tobias judges, Vorneth comforts.
He is the priest of death — humble, patient, and dutiful. His words are few, but his compassion is unshakable. He teaches mortals that death must be honored, not feared.

His creed:

> “The body is a vessel; the soul, a journey. Honor both.”



He despises necromancy and desecration above all things, viewing them as crimes against peace.


---

⚰️ Clergy and Temples

Priest Titles: Funerants, Pallbearers, Deathwrights

Temples:

Built with open courtyards and eternal fires where incense burns day and night.

Always beside graveyards or on high ground where wind carries away the scent of burning herbs.

Bells toll only once per soul — then never again.


Common Rituals:

The Rite of Ash: The body is anointed with oils, wrapped in linen, and burned or buried with a whisper: “Return to stillness.”

The Vigil of Seven Flames: Seven candles are lit for seven days, each symbolizing a virtue of life (honor, love, duty, truth, courage, mercy, and peace).

The Stone Blessing: Gravestones are carved with the deceased’s virtues — never their failures.



---

🩸 Artifacts and Relics

Censer of Eternal Rest: When swung, it soothes restless undead and seals cursed graves.

Ashen Mantle: Grants the wearer immunity to necrotic decay and the power to purify corrupted remains.

Tear of Vorneth: A crystal said to be formed from the god’s only tear — restores peace to tormented spirits.



---

⚔️ Divine Relationships

Deity	Relationship

Tobias	His master and judge; Vorneth enforces Tobias’s rulings upon mortal remains.
Nymera	His friend and co-worker; she guides souls, he guards their bodies.
Elyndra	Respectful allies; she renews what he releases.
Kelriss	Allies of necessity; where Kelriss decays, Vorneth sanctifies.
Marvok	Eternal enemy; Vorneth’s priests hunt his necromantic cults relentlessly.



---

🕯️ Worshippers

Vorneth’s faithful include:

Gravekeepers and undertakers

Physicians and healers

Paladins who protect sacred grounds

Mourners who have lost loved ones

Monks who meditate upon impermanence


Common Prayer:

> “Light the flame, bear the name, let all rest in peace.”




---

⚱️ Legends & Lore

The Thousand Candles: It is said Vorneth once walked the mortal world, lighting a candle for every innocent life lost in war. Each flame burned for a century.

The First Burial: He taught mortals to bury their dead rather than leave them to the wilds, marking the dawn of civilization.

The Mourner’s Oath: His priests swear lifelong celibacy to remain free of earthly attachment; they serve every funeral as if it were their own.



---

🕯️ Role in the World

Vorneth represents closure and respect — his name is invoked in every burial, even by those who do not follow his faith. His priests often accompany armies and noble houses, ensuring proper funerals are granted to all — friend or foe alike.

Where Tobias judges the soul, Vorneth tends the body and consoles the living.
He bridges the divine and mortal realms — the hand that lowers the shroud.
